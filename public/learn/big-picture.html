The big picture, explained simply

The whole system as a story, with no jargon. If you only read one guide, read this one.

⏱ 8 min readGuide 2 of 47

The problem the app solves

At work, bugs live in Jira, a website where teams track tasks. A big project (an "epic") can have dozens of bugs attached, and every developer has bugs assigned to them across many projects. Jira can show these, but it takes many clicks, and it does not tap you on the shoulder when something important changes.

Jira Bug Dashboard does three things:

  1. Collects the bugs you care about (all bugs of an epic, or all bugs assigned to you) on one screen.
  2. Visualises them: charts, counts, age, filters, markers for what is new or stale.
  3. Watches them for you. When something changes (a new assignment, a comment, a reopen), an assistant called JARVIS tells you with a notification and a voice.

The story of one click

Explained simply: a restaurant

Think of the app as a restaurant.

  • You sit at a table. Your browser is the table: it is where the food (data) is served and where you look at the menu (the page).
  • The waiter is the server. You never walk into the kitchen yourself. You tell the waiter what you want, and the waiter goes and gets it.
  • The kitchen is Jira, where the real data lives. The kitchen only serves waiters who show the right badge (a token).
  • The waiter keeps a small notebook: the database. It remembers your preferences and what it already told you, so it does not repeat itself.
  • Every minute, the waiter walks past the kitchen and checks whether anything changed for you. If it did, the waiter sends you a note (a push notification), even if you went home.

Why there has to be a waiter

You might ask: why not let the browser talk to Jira directly? Two reasons.

  1. Browsers are not allowed to. A safety rule called CORS stops a web page from one website reading data from another website unless that other website explicitly agrees. Jira does not agree. So a page on workers.dev simply cannot read atlassian.net. (Full story: HTTP, cookies, CORS and same-origin.)
  2. Badges must stay secret. The token that unlocks your Jira data is as powerful as your password. If it lived in the page, any bug in the page, or any browser extension, could steal it. A server can keep it locked away.

Three ways to run the same app

The same web page can run in three settings (called editions):

EditionWho is the waiter?Where is the badge kept?
LocalA small program on your own computer (Node.js)A file in your home folder that only you can read
CloudA Cloudflare Worker on the internetEncrypted in Cloudflare's database, the key kept separately
DemoNobody: the page makes up sample dataThere is no badge; it never touches Jira

Details: Local, Cloud and Demo editions.

What happens every minute

In the Cloud edition, Cloudflare wakes the Worker every minute (a cron trigger). The Worker picks the users whose turn it is, asks Jira "what changed for this person since I last looked?", writes anything new into the database, and sends a push notification to the person's devices. The browser's service worker (a tiny helper that lives in the browser even when the tab is closed) shows the notification. If the dashboard tab is open, JARVIS also speaks it. The full journey: The background check and A notification's journey.
