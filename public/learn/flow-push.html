A notification's journey

From "Bob commented on your bug" in Jira to a notification on your screen and JARVIS speaking, crossing five systems, with the message encrypted end to end.

⏱ 10 min readGuide 23 of 47

The pieces

Subscribing (once per device)

  1. In JARVIS you click Enable. Inside that click the page asks for notification permission (browsers only allow it from a user action).
  2. The page calls registration.pushManager.subscribe({ userVisibleOnly: true, applicationServerKey }). The key is the app's public VAPID key.
  3. The browser contacts its push service and returns a subscription: an endpoint URL (unique to this browser) plus two keys (p256dh, auth) that only this browser can decrypt with.
  4. The page sends it to POST /api/push/subscribe. The Worker checks the endpoint is a real push service and stores it.

Sending (each time there is news)

  1. The monitor creates events and applies your rules (not your own, type enabled, not quiet hours in your time zone).
  2. pushPlan() groups them into at most 3 notifications.
  3. The payload (titles, bodies, and the events so open tabs can speak them) is encrypted with your browser's keys (RFC 8291, aes128gcm).
  4. The Worker POSTs it to your endpoint with a VAPID header (a JWT signed with the app's private key proving which app is sending), TTL: 86400 (keep for a day if your device is offline) and Urgency.
  5. 404/410 from the push service = subscription dead → deleted. Network failure = retry next minute (up to 6 hours).

Receiving

  1. The push service delivers to your browser; the browser wakes sw.js and gives it the decrypted payload.
  2. The service worker always shows the notifications (browsers revoke push access from sites that receive pushes without showing anything).
  3. It posts the events to every open dashboard tab. The tab adds them to history, refreshes For You, and JARVIS speaks (skipping its own desktop notification, since one was already shown).
  4. Clicking a notification focuses an open tab on that bug, or opens /?view=foryou&issue=ABC-1.

Who can read the message?

PartySees
The push service (Google, Apple…)an encrypted blob, its size and the sending app's public key; not the content
Your browserthe content (it holds the private key)
Other users of the appnothing; subscriptions are per user and per device
