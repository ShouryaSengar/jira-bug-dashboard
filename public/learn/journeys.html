User journeys and use cases

Who uses the app, what they want to achieve, and the path they take. Engineers call this "use cases"; it is the bridge between the product and the design.

⏱ 8 min readGuide 5 of 47

The people (actors)

ActorWants to…
Developersee every bug assigned to them, notice changes immediately, avoid missing a reopened bug
Tech lead / QA leadsee all bugs of an epic, their age and status, who holds them, and how the backlog trends
Visitortry the app with sample data before connecting anything
Operator (you, hosting the Cloud edition)deploy, monitor, keep costs at zero, respond to privacy duties
Atlassian (a system, not a person)authenticate users, serve Jira data, ask for personal-data reports
Cloudflare (a system)run the code, store data, wake the monitor every minute

Use case diagram

Journey 1: a developer's first day with the Cloud edition

  1. Opens the link a colleague shared. Sees the landing page with "Sign in with Atlassian" and "Try with sample data".
  2. Signs in. Atlassian asks which Jira site to share and shows the read-only permissions. They click Accept. (What happens underneath.)
  3. Lands on For You, greeted by name, seeing every bug assigned to them.
  4. Opens JARVIS, clicks Enable for background notifications, allows the browser prompt, presses Test announcement and hears the voice.
  5. Closes the laptop. Within minutes of a colleague reopening one of their bugs, their phone or desktop shows "ABC-123 was reopened". (The notification's journey.)
  6. Next morning they open the dashboard; JARVIS says "Good morning. While you were away, 3 things happened across 2 of your bugs."

Journey 2: a lead reviewing an epic

  1. Switches to Epics, pastes the epic's Jira URL into the Track box.
  2. Reads the progress bar and KPIs: 34 open, 9 older than 7 days, 4 unassigned.
  3. Clicks the Unassigned tile: the table filters. Clicks 2 - Major in the severity chart: filters further.
  4. Exports the result as CSV for the stand-up.
  5. Presses Mark all as seen. Tomorrow, NEW and UPDATED markers show exactly what changed overnight.

Journey 3: a visitor trying the demo

Opens the GitHub Pages site. With no server available, the page detects demo mode and generates sample data in the browser. Every feature works, including simulated JARVIS events, so the visitor can hear a briefing without connecting anything.

Journey 4: the operator

Deploys with Wrangler, sets secrets, registers the Atlassian app, watches logs with wrangler tail, checks CPU metrics, and lets the weekly personal-data report run. All of it is described in Deploy the Cloud edition and Operating in production.

What this tells the design

Need from the journeysDesign consequence
Notifications when the laptop is closedMonitoring must run on a server on a schedule (cron), not in the page; delivery needs Web Push and a service worker
Anyone can sign in, nothing to installA hosted backend with OAuth, multi-user isolation, encrypted tokens
Try before connectingA demo mode that needs no server at all
Never miss a reopen, never repeatChange detection from the changelog with stable ids, not from timestamps
Free to hostFit inside Cloudflare's free limits: small, incremental work per minute
